In this thesis, we give a direct proof of a key geometric result in Deligne's approach to geometric class field theory.
We work primarily over a perfect field $k$. Let $C$ be a smooth, projective, geometrically connected curve over $k$, of genus $g$.
Let $\mdls=\sum_i n_iP_i$ be an effective divisor on $C$, called a modulus, and set $U=C\setminus \mdls$. Geometric class field theory asserts that every rank-one local system $\cF$ on $U$ whose ramification is bounded by $\mdls$ arises uniquely as the pullback of a multiplicative rank-one local system on
$\PicCm$ along the Abel--Jacobi morphism:
\[
\Phi:U\longrightarrow\PicCm.
\]
One of the main geometric ingredients in Deligne's approach to this assertion is the analysis of the symmetric powers $\cF^{[d]}$ on $U^{(d)}$, and in particular the proof that, for all sufficiently large $d$, they descend along
the degree $d$ Abel-Jacobi morphism:
\[
\Phi_d:U^{(d)}\longrightarrow\PicCm[d].
\]
The geometric-fibers of $\Phi_d$ (for $d \geq \deg \mdls +  2g - 1$) over any point are isomorphic to 
$$
\begin{cases}
\mathbb{A}_{k^{sep}}^{d - \deg \mdls - g + 1} & \text{if } \mdls > 0 \\
\mathbb{P}_{k^{sep}}^{d - g} & \text{if } \mdls = 0
\end{cases}
$$
Where $g$ is the genus of the curve $C$.
The unramified case ($\mdls = 0$) is relatively simple, as the Abel-Jacobi map is proper, surjective with geometrically connected fibers, which follows from the fact that it is a fibration in projective spaces.
Thus, by using the homotopy exact sequence for the \'etale fundamental group,
one gets an isomorphism between the \'etale fundamental group of $U^{(d)} (=C^{(d)})$ and that of $\PicCm[d] (=\PicC[d])$.

The ramified case ($\mdls > 0$) is more subtle, as the Abel-Jacobi map is not proper anymore, and one needs to analyze the ramification of $\cF^{[d]}$ "along the boundary" of $U^{(d)}$ in $C^{(d)}$.

Previous work has generalized Deligne's approach to the ramified case, 
most notably by Guignard \cite{Guignard2018} and Takeuchi \cite{takeuchi2019blow}. 
Their approaches differ. 
To descend, Guignard proves that the restriction of $\cF^{[d]}$ to any line in the fiber of the degree $d$ Abel-Jacobi map is a constant 
étale sheaf. He achieves this by demonstrating that the restriction is at most tamely ramified and invoking the triviality of the tame fundamental group 
of $\mathbb{A}^1_k$. His analysis relies on local geometric class field theory. 
It is also worth noting that Guignard's method generalizes to relative curves over arbitrary base schemes.
Takeuchi, on the other hand, constructs a compactification of $U^{(d)}$ by blowing up $C^{(d)}$ along certain well-chosen centers.
This compactification, denoted by $\tilde{C}^{(d)}_\mdls$, has $U^{(d)}$ as an open subscheme with a codimension 1 closed subscheme $H$ as complement. 
He then shows that the Abel-Jacobi map extends to a proper morphism from $\tilde{C}^{(d)}_\mdls$ to $\PicCm[d]$,
which is a fibration in projective spaces. Thus, by the homotopy exact sequence for the \'etale fundamental group, one gets an isomorphism between the \'etale fundamental group of
$\tilde{C}^{(d)}_\mdls$ and that of $\PicCm[d]$. To conclude the descent, Takeuchi analyzes the ramification of $\cF^{[d]}$ along the boundary
$H=\tilde{C}^{(d)}_\mdls \setminus U^{(d)}$ of $U^{(d)}$ in $\tilde{C}^{(d)}_\mdls$,
showing that it is tamely ramified there, which suffices. His method relies on the theory of refined Swan conductors.
% \textcolor{red}{[incorporate \cite{Rabinoff2014Witt} here]}.

For an account of these approaches, see \cite{Guignard2018} and \cite{takeuchi2019blow}.
For a full approach following Deligne's method in the unramified case, and the tamely ramified case see \cite{tendler2015geometricclassfieldtheory}, and \cite{Toth2011}.

\textit{In this thesis}, we take a more elementary approach and calculate the ramification of $\cF^{[d]}$ at the generic point of $H$ directly, using explicit ramification theory rather than refined Swan conductors.
We first reduce the problem to cyclic torsors. We then treat cyclic groups of order prime to the characteristic using Kummer theory and, in characteristic $p>0$, cyclic groups of order $p$ and $p^r$, with $r\geq2$, using Artin--Schreier and Artin--Schreier--Witt theory, respectively.
These descriptions provide explicit local equations, which allow us to analyze the relevant valuations after blowing up and prove that the ramification is at most tame.
To prove geometric class field theory for curves, 
we utilize Takeuchi's construction of the compactification $\tilde{C}^{(d)}_\mdls$ 
of $U^{(d)}$ via the blowing up of $C^{(d)}$.

We now introduce the notation needed to state geometric class field theory, its standard degree-$d$ reduction, and the ramification theorem proved in this thesis.

Let $k$ be a perfect field, and let $C$ be a projective smooth geometrically connected curve over $k$, with genus $g$. 

Fix a modulus $\mdls$, i.e.\ an effective Cartier divisor of $C$ and let $U$ be its complement in $C$. 
The pairs $(\cL, \alpha)$, where $\cL$ is an invertible $\cO_C$-module and $\alpha:\cO_\mdls\xrightarrow{\sim}\cL|_\mdls$ is a rigidification of $\cL$ along $\mdls$, are parametrized by a $k$-group scheme $\PicCm$, called the rigidified Picard scheme.
We write $\PicCm[d]$ for its degree-$d$ component.
The Abel-Jacobi morphism $$ \Phi : U \to \PicCm $$
is the morphism which sends a section $x$ of $U$ to the pair $(\cO_C(x), 1)$. 

Geometric class field theory asserts that rank-one local systems on $U$, with ramification bounded by $\mdls$, arise uniquely as pullbacks of multiplicative rank-one local systems on $\PicCm$.
For a finite commutative coefficient ring $\Lambda$, the $\Lambda^\times$-torsor associated with such a multiplicative local system is an isogeny onto $\PicCm$ with finite constant kernel $\Lambda^\times$.
The isogeny formulation will not play a direct role in our argument, which instead focuses on the degree-$d$ descent statement introduced below.

The fundamental result of GCFT can be formulated as:
\begin{theorem}[Geometric Class Field Theory]\label{thm:GCFT}
    Let $\Lambda$ be a finite commutative ring of cardinality invertible in $k$, and let $\cF$ be an \'etale sheaf of $\Lambda$-modules, locally free of rank 1 on $U$, with ramification bounded by $\mdls$. 
    Then, there exists a unique (up to isomorphism) 
    multiplicative\footnote{Here multiplicative means that the local system is equipped with an isomorphism compatible with the group law on $\PicCm$, satisfying the usual associativity and unit conditions; see \cite{Guignard2018}.} 
\'etale sheaf of $\Lambda$-modules $\cG$ on $\PicCm$, locally free of rank 1, such that the pullback of $\cG$ by $\Phi$ is isomorphic to $\cF$.
\end{theorem}

Let $d$ be a positive integer. 
We denote by $U^{(d)}$ the $d$-th symmetric power of $U$ over $k$. 
For an \'etale sheaf $\cF$ on $U_{\text{\'et}}$, we denote by $\cF^{[d]}$ the $d$-th symmetric power of $\cF$ on $U^{(d)}$. 
The degree-$d$ Abel--Jacobi morphism is
\[
\Phi_d:U^{(d)}\longrightarrow\PicCm[d],
\]
sending an effective divisor $D=x_1+\cdots+x_d$ on $U$ to the pair
\[
\bigl(\cO_C(D),1\bigr).
\]

A standard symmetric-power argument\footnote{See the last page of \cite{Guignard2018}, Section 8.3 of \cite{tendler2015geometricclassfieldtheory}, or the proof of Theorem 1.2 in \cite{takeuchi2019blow} for details on this reduction.} reduces \Cref{thm:GCFT} to the following problem: for every sufficiently large $d$, construct a rank-one local system $\cG_d$ on $\PicCm[d]$ whose pullback along $\Phi_d$ is isomorphic to $\cF^{[d]}$.
In our setting, pullback along $\Phi_d$ is fully faithful, so any such local system is automatically unique up to isomorphism.
Moreover, the canonical compatibility of the symmetric powers $\cF^{[d]}$ under the addition of effective divisors descends uniquely to the family $\{\cG_d\}$.
The resulting multiplicative family determines the multiplicative local system on $\PicCm$ appearing in \Cref{thm:GCFT}.
Consequently, it suffices to prove the following reduced statement:

\begin{restatable}{theorem}{GCFT_reduced}\label{thm:GCFT_reduced}
    Let $\Lambda$ be a finite commutative ring of cardinality invertible in $k$, and let $\cF$ be an \'etale sheaf of $\Lambda$-modules, locally free of rank 1 on $U_{\text{\'et}}$, with ramification bounded by $\mdls$. 
    Then, for sufficiently large integer $d$, there exists a unique (up to isomorphism) \'etale sheaf of $\Lambda$-modules $\cG_d$ on $\PicCm[d]$, locally free of rank 1, such that the pullback of $\cG_d$ by $\Phi_d$ is isomorphic to $\cF^{[d]}$.
\end{restatable}

The main technical input in the proof of \Cref{thm:GCFT_reduced} is the following local ramification theorem:

\begin{restatable}{theorem}{SymmetricPowerOfSheavesIsTamelyRamifiedReduction}\label{theorem:SymmetricPowerOfSheavesIsTamelyRamifiedReduction}
     Let $\Lambda$ be a finite commutative ring of cardinality invertible in $k$, and let $\cF$ be an \'etale sheaf of $\Lambda$-modules, locally free of rank 1 on $U_{\text{\'et}}$, with ramification bounded by $\mdls = \sum n_i P_i$. 
     Let $\ndls = n_i P_i \subset \mdls$ be a single-point submodulus of $\mdls$.
     Then $\cF^{[\deg \ndls]}$ is tamely ramified at the generic point $\eta_\ndls$ of the exceptional divisor $E_\ndls$ 
     of the blowup $X_{\ndls}$ of $C^{(\deg \ndls)}$ at the closed point $\ndls$.
\end{restatable}

As a final methodological note, for convenience we carry out the ramification calculations in the language of $G$-torsors, with $G=\Lambda^\times$, rather than rank-one local systems.
By \Cref{prop:torsor_module_equivalence}, the two formulations are equivalent, and the results translate directly between them.


The thesis is organized as follows:

\begin{description}
    \item[Chapter 1 - General Preliminaries] establishes the equivalence between rank-one local systems and $G=\Lambda^\times$-torsors and develops the torsor operations, finite \'etale covers, and symmetric-power constructions used throughout the thesis. It also collects the basic geometric facts about symmetric powers and blowups needed in the subsequent ramification calculations.
    \item[Chapter 2 - Ramification After Blowup Is Tame] proves \Cref{theorem:SymmetricPowerOfSheavesIsTamelyRamifiedReduction}. After recalling the necessary ramification theory for discrete valuations and finite abelian torsors, it reduces the problem to cyclic torsors on $\mathbb P^1$ and treats the Kummer, Artin--Schreier, and Artin--Schreier--Witt cases through explicit calculations at the exceptional divisor.
    \item[Chapter 3 - Geometric Class Field Theory] deduces \Cref{thm:GCFT_reduced} from the ramification theorem proved in Chapter~2. It recalls the generalized Picard scheme and the Abel--Jacobi morphism, introduces Takeuchi's compactification, and combines the single-point calculations to establish tameness along the full boundary $H$. It then extends $\cF^{[d]}$ across the boundary and descends the resulting local system to $\PicCm[d]$.
    \item[Appendix A] \emph{Cyclic Torsor Theories and Witt Vectors} collects the group-scheme constructions, classification results, normal forms, and proofs supporting the three cyclic ramification theories used in Chapter~2.
    \item[Appendix B] \emph{A Failure of an Artin--Schreier Induction Strategy} examines the tempting attempt to deduce the cyclic $p$-power calculation inductively from the degree-$p$ Artin--Schreier case, and explains how wild base change obstructs that argument.
\end{description}
