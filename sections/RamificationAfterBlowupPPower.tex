\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Case (3): Artin--Schreier--Witt.}
We use a direct Artin--Schreier--Witt calculation;
Appendix~\ref{appendix:artin_schreier_induction_failure} explains why
induction through the degree-$p$ layers does not suffice after the intervening
wild base change.

The common reduction replaces the original group $\Z/p^r\Z$ by the subgroup
$H=\operatorname{im}(\rho)$, of order $p^s$ for some $1\leq s\leq r$, and
renames $H$ as $G$. Case~(2) treats $s=1$, so we may assume that
$G=\Z/p^s\Z$ with $s\geq2$.

With $R=k[x,x^{-1}]$ and $U=\bG_m=\Spec R$ as above,
\Cref{lemma:realization_over_Gm}(3) presents the realized torsor
$\cP\to U$ as
\[
    \wp\vec X=\vec f=(f_0,\dots,f_{s-1}),
    \qquad f_j\in x^{-1}k[x^{-1}],
\]
where, on setting $m_j=\deg_{x^{-1}}f_j$ for $f_j\ne0$ and $m_j=0$ for
$f_j=0$, one has
\[
    p^{s-1-j}m_j<d\qquad(0\leq j<s).
\]
Equivalently, $\cP$ is the pullback of the finite \'etale homomorphism
\[
    \wp=F-\mathrm{id}:\bW_s\longrightarrow\bW_s,
\]
whose kernel is $W_s(\F_p)\cong G$, along the section
$\vec f\in\bW_s(U)=W_s(R)$. Put
\[
    S:=R[\vec X]/(\wp\vec X\ominus\vec f).
\]
Then $\cP\cong\Spec S$. As in the previous cases, the contracted product has
coordinate algebra $(S^{\otimes_k d})^{\KG}$, where
$\KG:=\ker(G^d\to G)$. Write
$\vec X_i$ for the pullback of $\vec X$ from the $i$-th factor. Since the
group law on $\bW_s$ is Witt addition,
\Cref{prop:parallel_cyclic_product_torsor} shows that the defining sections
$\vec f(x_i)$ add, giving the equation
\[
    \wp\vec Y=\vec\alpha,
    \qquad
    \vec\alpha:=\bigoplus_{i=1}^d\vec f(x_i).
\]
Here $\vec Y$ is the Witt-vector coordinate on the contracted product, and
its pullback to the product torsor is
$\bigoplus_{i=1}^d\vec X_i$. Consequently,
\[
    (S^{\otimes_k d})^{\KG}
       \cong
    R^{\otimes_k d}[\vec Y]/(\wp\vec Y\ominus\vec\alpha).
\]
The $S_d$-descent follows the same pattern as in Cases~(1) and~(2): the group
$S_d$ fixes $\vec Y$ and $\vec\alpha$ componentwise, and
\Cref{lemma:asw_etale_torsor} supplies an $S_d$-invariant basis over
$R^{\otimes_k d}$, so taking invariants acts only on the coefficients. Thus
\Cref{prop:symmetric_power_semidirect_quotient} gives
\[
    \cP^{[d]}\cong
    \Spec\frac{k[e_1,\dots,e_d,e_d^{-1}][\vec Y]}
                   {(\wp\vec Y\ominus\vec\alpha)}.
\]

Because Witt addition is given by universal polynomials, each component
$\alpha_n$ is a Laurent polynomial, and commutativity makes it invariant
under permutations of the indices.
It remains to examine the components of $\vec\alpha$ at the exceptional
valuation. The Witt components are not ordinary componentwise sums, so the
degree count must respect the Witt weights. Give $f_j(x_i)$ weight $p^j$.
By \Cref{lemma:witt_addition_isobaric}, every nonzero monomial
\[
    \prod_{i,j}f_j(x_i)^{a_{ij}}
\]
appearing in $\alpha_n$ satisfies
\[
    \sum_{i,j}a_{ij}p^j=p^n.
\]
Put $A_j:=\sum_i a_{ij}$ and $y_i:=x_i^{-1}$. Since $f_j(x_i)$ is a
polynomial in $y_i$ of degree $m_j$, every monomial obtained after expansion
has total degree at most $\sum_jA_jm_j$. The pole bounds therefore give
\[
    \sum_jA_jm_j
       <d\sum_jA_jp^{j-(s-1)}
       =d\,p^{n-(s-1)}
       \leq d.
\]
The first inequality is strict because a nonzero monomial contains some
nonzero $f_j$, whose pole bound is strict. Thus every monomial in $\alpha_n$
has total degree strictly less than $d$, and
\Cref{lemma:symmetric_regularity} gives
\[
    \alpha_n\in\kappa\qquad(0\leq n<s),
\]
and therefore
\[
    \vec\alpha\in W_s(\kappa)\subset W_s(\widehat\Oo_\eta).
\]

Set $A:=\widehat\Oo_\eta$ and
\[
    B:=A[\vec Y]/(\wp\vec Y\ominus\vec\alpha).
\]
By \Cref{lemma:asw_etale_torsor}, the algebra $B$ is finite \'etale over
$A$. After base change to $\widehat K_\eta=\kappa((e_d))$, we obtain
\[
    \cP^{[d]}\big|_{\Spec\widehat K_\eta}
       \cong
    \Spec\bigl(B\otimes_A\widehat K_\eta\bigr)
       =
    \Spec\frac{\widehat K_\eta[\vec Y]}
                   {(\wp\vec Y\ominus\vec\alpha)}.
\]
Every field factor is therefore unramified over $\widehat K_\eta$. Hence
$\cP^{[d]}$ is unramified at $\eta$, proving part~(3).
