We calculate the ramication of the symmetric product F
                                                          [deg m] of a local system F on a curve C \m.

Assuming the ramication of F is bounded by m, we nd that the symmetric product F
                                                                                           [deg m] is at

most tamely ramied at the generic point of the exceptional divisor Em of the blowup of C
                                                                                             (deg m) at

m. As a primary application, we utilize this result to prove geometric class eld theory for curves.
Our approach builds upon the geometric framework for the unramied case originally established
by Deligne for the rank-one Langlands correspondence, following the subsequent extensions to the
ramied case developed by Takeuchi and Guignard.

General Preliminaries

This chapter collects the constructions and conventions used in both main parts of the thesis. Rank-
one étale local systems are the natural objects in the statement of geometric class eld theory.
For the geometric and ramication-theoretic arguments, however, it is more convenient to work
with torsors: they are represented by nite étale covers, admit concrete contracted products and
quotients, and make ramication accessible through extensions of local elds.

                                                ×
  If Λ is a nite commutative ring and G = Λ , the sheaf of frames of a rank-one Λ-local system is a
G-torsor, and conversely a G-torsor determines an associated rank-one local system. We record this
equivalence once below. Thereafter, the main results are stated in the language of local systems,
while the geometric and ramication calculations are carried out with the corresponding torsors.




1.1     Torsors and Rank-One Local Systems
In what follows, we largely adhere to the treatment of torsors and group objects found in published
notes of Alex Youcis [You20]. Let C = (C, J) be a site and let E = Sh(C ) be the associated topos.
Let G be a group object in E . We denote by GE the category of objects in E endowed with a left
G -action.
Denition 4.      A G   -torsor in E is an object P of GE satisfying the following conditions:

   1. The structural morphism P → 1 is an epimorphism in E (i.e., P is locally non-empty).


   2. The map G × P → P × P dened by (g, p) 7→ (g · p, p) is an isomorphism in E (i.e., G acts
      simply transitively on P ).


  Since E is the topos of sheaves on a site C , the denition can be reformulated in terms of covers.
A G -sheaf P is a G -torsor if:


   1. For every object X ∈ C , there exists a covering {Ui → X} ∈ J such that P(Ui ) ̸= ∅ for all i.


   2. For any X ∈ C where P(X) is non-empty, the action of G(X) on P(X) is simply transitively.


  A fundamental property of torsors is their local triviality: a G -sheaf P is a G -torsor if and only if
it is locally isomorphic to the trivial torsor. Specically, for every X ∈ C , there must exist a cover



                                                    8
{Ui → X} such that the restriction P|Ui is isomorphic, as a G|Ui -sheaf, to G|Ui acting on itself by
left multiplication.

  A   morphism of G -torsors f : P1 → P2 is a morphism of sheaves that is equivariant with respect
to the G -action.

Proposition 5. Every morphism of G -torsors is an isomorphism. Consequently, the category of
G -torsors in E forms a groupoid.

Proof. Locally on a cover trivializing both torsors, such a morphism is a G -equivariant endomor-

being an isomorphism may be checked on a cover.

2.3     Reduction to Cyclic Torsors on P1
Before introducing explicit equations, we explain the strategy of the proof and isolate two reductions
that simplify the situation. The theorem concerns a torsor under an arbitrary nite abelian group
on an arbitrary smooth curve, whereas the calculation we ultimately wish to perform is local and
cyclic. We show that both the structure group and the geometry may be simplied without changing
the ramication problem relevant to the exceptional divisor.

  The rst reduction is algebraic: using the decomposition of a nite abelian group into cyclic fac-
tors, together with the compatibility of the symmetric-power construction and ramication bounds
with products and quotients, we reduce the theorem to cyclic groups of order prime to p and cyclic
                  r
groups of order p . This explains why the three cyclic theories recalled in the next section suce.
The second reduction is geometric and formallocal: the ramication of the induced torsor at the
generic point of the exceptional divisor depends only on the restriction of the original torsor to the
punctured formal neighbourhood of the point supporting the modulus. It therefore tells us exactly
what the cyclic theories must accomplish: realize the resulting local cyclic torsor by an explicit
equation on Gm ⊂ Pk .

  Together, these reductions transform the original problem into three parallel explicit calculations,
corresponding to Kummer, ArtinSchreier, and ArtinSchreierWitt theory. The following section
recalls precisely the conclusions from these theories that provide the required realizations.

B.5     What the example proves
The example does not contradict part (3) of Theorem 58. That theorem is proved in Chapter 2 by
a direct ArtinSchreierWitt calculation. Rather, the example isolates the missing implication in
the proposed alternative proof:

  3. a wild base change can absorb the ramication of E/F , so unramiedness of EKC ′ /KC ′ does
      not recover unramiedness of E/F ;
