\chapter{A Failure of an Artin--Schreier Induction Strategy}
\label{appendix:artin_schreier_induction_failure}
\label{section:a_wrong_path}
Part~(2) of \Cref{theorem:cyclic_cases_after_blowup} proves the order-$p$
case by an explicit Artin--Schreier calculation. Part~(3) treats
$G=\Z/p^r\Z$ by working directly with an Artin--Schreier--Witt equation.
It is natural to ask whether the second calculation can instead be obtained
by iterating the first.

Indeed, let
\[
G=\Z/p^r\Z,\qquad
H=p^{r-1}G\cong\Z/p\Z,\qquad
G/H\cong\Z/p^{r-1}\Z,
\]
and factor a $G$-torsor $\cP\to U$ as
\[
\cP\longrightarrow\cP/H\longrightarrow U.
\]
The tempting induction has three steps:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item apply the induction hypothesis to the $G/H$-torsor
        $\cP/H\to U$;
  \item regard $\cP\to\cP/H$ as an $H\cong\Z/p\Z$-torsor and apply the
        Artin--Schreier calculation after compactifying its base;
  \item combine the two unramifiedness statements to recover the result for
        the original $G$-torsor.
\end{enumerate}

{\color{red}
The first two steps do produce natural statements, but they live over
different exceptional valuations. Passing from one to the other involves a
base change induced by the comparison between the symmetric power of a
torsor as a scheme and its symmetric power as a torsor. That base change can
be wildly ramified. The purpose of this appendix is to formulate the proposed
induction precisely, follow both inputs through the resulting valuation
tower, and explain why they do not by themselves imply the required
conclusion.
\par}
{\color{blue}
The first two steps suggest natural unramifiedness statements, but they
refer to different exceptional valuations. There are two obstructions to
combining them.
\begin{enumerate}
\item First, the ordinary exceptional valuation on the symmetric
power of the covering curve need not restrict to the ordinary exceptional
valuation on the symmetric power of the original curve. Equivalently,
the generic point $\eta_{C'}$ need not lie above $\eta_C$, so the two
chosen exceptional valuations cannot in general be compared in a tower.
\item Second, even when
these valuations are compatible, the comparison between symmetric powers
of schemes and symmetric powers of torsors can induce a wildly ramified
base change, after which unramifiedness need not descend. Even granting
both proposed unramifiedness statements, their combination does not
formally establish the induction step.
\end{enumerate}
We first exhibit
the valuation mismatch, and then analyze the second obstruction under an
explicit compatibility hypothesis.
\par}
